02 HISTORY OF AI

Explain key moments in the historical development of AI and ML and relate them to modern AI systems.

AI has developed from systems that followed rules written by people to perform narrow tasks, to systems that learn patterns from data. As computer software and hardware became more powerful, and more data became available, machine learning allowed AI systems to handle more complex tasks. Modern tools reflect these different stages of AI development.

Translation Tools

The progression of AI systems can be seen in the development of translation tools. Early, rule-based systems relied on dictionaries and grammar instructions written by people. Later, systems used machine learning to study large collections of translated text and learn statistical patterns between languages. With the addition of neural networks, translation tools were advanced by processing sentences as a whole and learning relationships between words. Finally, newer transformer-based systems build on this progress by using context across a text to produce more natural translations.

This progression—from following human-written rules to learning patterns from data and using context—shows how AI methods have grown more sophisticated. Each stage expanded what translation systems could do and helped make translation faster and more accessible.

Email Spam Filters

Spam filters offer another example of AI’s development. Early models of email spam filters used hand-written rules to flag suspicious messages. As the system developed, filters based on statistics learned from examples of spam and actual emails, identifying patterns that could help classify a suspicious message.

Today, well-known email platforms like Gmail use spam filtering that is AI enhanced. This example shows how AI systems can move from rules that people implement to models that can learn patterns from data. This also signifies how machine learning tools can make life more convenient.

03 ETHICS OF AI

Identify and discuss ethical issues associated with AI and ML and their societal implications.

With the constant rise of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in today’s day and age, there will always be cause for concern regarding the ethical ways that society uses AI. Two examples show how these tools can raise concerns about privacy, consent, and fairness.

Facial Recognition in an Apartment Building

In 2018, a landlord in Brooklyn proposed installing facial recognition cameras at an apartment building entrance. Tenants and their guests would need to submit face scans to enter. However, residents organized against the proposal and succeeded in stopping it.

This proposal raised concerns about privacy and consent. A face is sensitive, personal data because a person can not easily change it like they could with a password. Residents also might not know how long their images are kept and who can access them. Additionally, if the system failed to recognize a tenant, that person could potentially be locked out of their own home.

This issue is significant because people depend on their homes for safety and shelter, while landlords control access to their buildings. While this method of entry might make lives more convenient, that benefit should be weighed against the risk of the collection of such sensitive information and data. A safer way that landlords could implement this method could be to clearly tell the tenants what information is collected, how it will be used, and how it will be protected. They should also have an alternative way to enter the building to avoid complications with lock outs.

Speech-to-Text Systems

Automated speech recognition systems use machine learning to turn spoken words into text. They are used for tasks such as creating captions and voice dictation. In 2020, a study was conducted that tested five speech-to-text systems and found that they made significantly more transcription errors for black speakers than white speakers.

This situation raises an ethical issue for fairness. If a system is less accurate for some speakers, especially by race, they may have a harder time using voice related technology or receiving accurate transcriptions. Errors could leave out or change important information, especially if an individual heavily relies on accurate transcriptions. This 2020 study shows that even though AI tools are widely accessible, they may work less well for some groups.

In my opinion, I believe that speech-to-text systems should be tested with speakers from different backgrounds so that they can improve the systems to function properly with all ethnic groups. Organizations that create these systems should also let people review and correct important transcriptions. These steps could make speech recognition tools more reliable and fair for all people.

04 CORE TECHNIQUES OF AI

Describe how core AI techniques such as search and adversarial reasoning are used to model intelligent behavior.

Search and adversarial reasoning are two techniques AI systems use to model intelligent behavior. Search helps an AI explore possible actions and plan toward a goal, while adversarial reasoning helps it choose actions while opposing another player.

Search: Tree of Thoughts

Search can help AI systems explore possible ways to solve a problem. One example is Tree of Thoughts, which is a framework for AI that is developed for large language models. Instead of producing one chain of steps, the system can generate several possible next steps and explore the ones that seem the most promising. Each branch in the Tree of Thoughts represents a possible direction toward a solution.

Tree of Thoughts can use search algorithms such as Breadth-First Search (BFS) and Depth-First Search (DFS) to decide which branches to further examine. This can help an AI agent to work through problems that require heavy planning and decision making.

Adversarial Reasoning: Tic-Tac-Toe

Adversarial reasoning is useful when an AI agent is competing against an opponent. Tic-tac-toe is an example of this. The AI can map out the different board layouts, or game states, that could result from possible moves. Each node represents a game state, and each branch represents a legal move, or action.

The AI can use minimax to select a move that maximizes its outcome while assuming the opponent will choose moves that minimize it. This lets the AI plan ahead–a move that looks good now may not be the most optimal move in winning the game. In this way, the AI considers possible future outcomes and anticipates how the opposing player will respond.
